An exact ledger of meaning in messages, with measurements on the genetic code and on English.

Short answer. Latitude is the freedom a speaker keeps in wording a fixed meaning, H(X|W). Doubt is what a listener cannot recover about the meaning from the wording, H(W|X). With the information the wording conveys, they add up exactly to the entropy of the message.

The columns behind the title are the ledger of a small lexicon (paper, Figure 1). The title turns white where it crosses the black column, the information the form conveys.

The ledger

In 1948 Shannon set meaning aside: the semantic aspects of communication, he wrote, are irrelevant to the engineering problem. That describes a problem, not the world. This paper keeps the books on meaning too.

Take a message with a meaning W (what is meant) and a form X (what is said). Every language has synonymy, many forms for one meaning, and ambiguity, one form for many meanings. The entropy of the form, H(X), mixes the two. The ledger separates them.

E = I + Λ   C = I + Δ   E − C = Λ − Δ

C = H(W) is the content, E = H(X) the expression, I = I(W;X) the conveyance, Λ = H(X|W) the latitude and Δ = H(W|X) the doubt. The identities are the chain rule (Theorem 1). Reading in head-counts: 2Λ is the effective number of ways a meaning is said, 2Δ the effective number of meanings a form is taken to have.

What it costs

Saying instead of meaning costs Λ bits per symbol, whatever the doubt (Theorem 7). Sending the forms and then resolving the meaning takes n(E+Δ) bits, which is nC + nΛ.

Vocabulary law (Corollary 2). An unambiguous language with content C and latitude Λ needs at least 2C+Λ distinct forms. Each bit of freedom doubles the vocabulary. Drag the sliders; the defaults are E. coli codons.


Meaning without an annotator

Who decides what W is? Nobody has to. Pick a purpose Y, whatever the receiver will act on. The meaning of a message for Y is the belief it leaves behind, P(Y | x).

By Theorem 4 that belief is sufficient (it keeps all of I(X;Y)), minimal (the coarsest partition that loses nothing about Y) and monotone (weaker purposes give coarser meanings).

Leakage, I(X;Y | W), tests a declared meaning against a purpose: how much the wording still says about consequences. Theorem 5(d): only an injective map is adequate for every purpose. There is no purpose-free meaning, and each step of abstraction leaks exactly the relevance it discards.

The genetic code: doubt is zero

A ribosome translates each codon to one amino acid, so Δ = 0 and E = C + Λ exactly. Of the 64 codons, about 21 meanings leave room to choose. For E. coli K-12: C = 4.175, Λ = 1.501, E = 5.676 bits per codon. The effective vocabulary 2E is 51.1 of 64 codons, and sending the protein instead of the DNA saves 26.5% of the entropy.

Where identity lives

Pick a codon from one of six genomes. It carries 0.178 bits about which genome it came from. Proposition 6 splits that over three levels of meaning, and the largest part sits in the finest one, the synonymous choice, which is exactly the leakage of the amino acid.

Latitude is a channel

What the speaker may choose without changing the meaning can carry a second message. In an idealized model with a shared key, the capacity of that channel is exactly Λ bits per symbol (Theorem 8). The converse holds for every scheme; the scheme in the proof reaches it and never outputs a wrong message, only a detected failure.

On 240 real E. coli genes, the amino-acid sequence of each gene was kept and the codons were chosen to spell a hidden payload. All 240 decoded correctly. The first 16 codons of one of them, with the codons that changed highlighted:

English: synonymy and ambiguity together

In English neither map is a function. On the well-attested core of SemCor (every lemma and synset seen at least 20 times), doubt exceeds latitude for lemmas by 0.44 bits per word. Change what counts as a form and the sign changes: with inflected words as the forms, latitude exceeds doubt.

Among 22 wordnets with at least 10,000 synsets, latitude exceeds doubt in 12 and doubt exceeds latitude in 10. Neither sign is a general property of lexicons; the corpus result is a statement about English usage under a stated definition of the form.

Proofs and checks

Theorem 1 in three lines. The chain rule gives H(W,X) = H(W) + H(X|W) = H(X) + H(W|X). Mutual information is I = H(W) − H(W|X) = H(X) − H(X|W), so E = I + Λ and C = I + Δ. Subtracting gives E − C = Λ − Δ. Δ = 0 exactly when W is a function of X.

Limits the paper states

  • A partition is not all of meaning: every meaning here is meaning for something.
  • Meaning-preserving is relative to the map; the embedding preserves proteins, not necessarily fitness.
  • The coding theorems assume independent draws; text and genes are not independent.
  • SemCor is one corpus of 1960s American English, and whole-corpus values have not converged.

Questions

What are latitude and doubt in information theory?

Latitude is Λ = H(X|W), the freedom a speaker keeps in choosing the form once the meaning is fixed (synonymy). Doubt is Δ = H(W|X), what a listener cannot recover about the meaning from the form (ambiguity).

What is the identity behind the ledger?

The chain rule gives E = I + Λ and C = I + Δ, where C = H(W) is content, E = H(X) is expression and I = I(W;X) is conveyance. Hence E − C = Λ − Δ: what is said exceeds what is meant by the speaker's latitude minus the listener's doubt.

How much latitude does the genetic code have?

Between 1.235 and 1.501 bits per codon across six sequenced genomes, which is 22.9 to 26.5 percent of codon entropy. For E. coli K-12, C = 4.175, Λ = 1.501 and E = 5.676 bits per codon.

Can a message be hidden in DNA without changing the protein?

Yes, in the model of the paper: with a shared key, latitude is the capacity of the channel. 90,127 bits were written into the synonymous codons of 240 real E. coli genes, all decoded correctly, with every amino-acid sequence unchanged. This is protein-preserving, not shown to be biologically neutral.

Is doubt larger than latitude in English?

It depends on what counts as a form. On the well-attested core of SemCor, doubt exceeds latitude for lemmas by 0.44 bits per word, but latitude exceeds doubt by 0.20 bits when inflected words are the forms. Among 22 wordnets, latitude exceeds doubt in 12.
