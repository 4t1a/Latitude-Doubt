\noindent{\sffamily\bfseries Abstract.}\enspace
Shannon set the meaning of messages aside as irrelevant to the engineering problem. This paper restores it as bookkeeping.
For a source that pairs what is meant, $W$, with what is said, $X$, the joint entropy divides exactly into three non-negative parts:
the information the form conveys, $I(W;X)$; the \emph{latitude} $\Lat=H(X\given W)$, which the speaker remains free to choose once the meaning is fixed;
and the \emph{doubt} $\Dbt=H(W\given X)$, which the listener cannot recover from the form. The identities are the chain rule; the use made of them is the contribution.
Meaning need not be declared by an annotator. For any purpose $Y$, the coarsest partition of messages that loses no information about $Y$ is the belief $P(Y\given x)$ that they induce,
and only the identity partition is adequate for every purpose. The \emph{leakage} $I(X;Y\given W)$ measures how much a declared meaning fails to screen off form from consequence.
Latitude has an operational value: in an idealized model with a shared key, it is the exact rate at which a hidden message can be written into the form of an unambiguous source without altering the meaning or the statistics of the text, and a finite-precision implementation is analysed separately.
An unambiguous language with content $C$ and latitude $\Lat$ needs at least $2^{C+\Lat}$ distinct forms.
We measure the ledger on six sequenced genomes, where the meaning map is the genetic code and the doubt is zero. Latitude is \SminGenome--\SmaxGenome{} bits per codon;
synonymous choice carries \PanelFrac\,\% of the information that identifies the genome of a codon and \HegFrac\,\% of the information that separates highly expressed \emph{E.\,coli} genes from the rest;
held-out genes reproduce these information values to within 0.003 bits; and \EmbBits{} bits were hidden in \EmbGenes{} real genes without changing the encoded amino-acid sequence of any of them.
In English the balance between latitude and doubt depends on what counts as a form and on whether items are weighted by use: on the well-attested core of SemCor doubt exceeds latitude for lemmas, by \SemDmSTwenty{} bits per word, but not for inflected words, and among \InvN{} wordnets latitude exceeds doubt in \InvLatGreater{}.
The estimators are validated against a law of known truth, and every number in the text is produced by the code that accompanies the paper.\par
